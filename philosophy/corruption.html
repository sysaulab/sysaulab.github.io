An Essay on Corruption

Mankind at war with itself.

On the myth of control, the market for influence, and the greed that eats us from the inside. Not a story about one villain — a story about a system that sells everything, including the ground we stand on.

The comforting myth

Let’s start with the story people tell. It goes like this: Israel controls the West. A small country, through lobbies and money and blackmail, has captured the United States, Britain, France — the whole bloc — and bends it to its will. Every war, every vote, every silence traces back to a single hidden hand.

It’s a satisfying story. It has a villain. It has a mechanism. It explains a lot.

It’s also wrong — not because Israel isn’t powerful, or because pro-Israel lobbies aren’t effective (they are), but because the story gets the system backwards. Israel did not capture a healthy political system and turn it rotten. It found a system already rotten and became one of its most skilled players.

The myth of a single controller is a comfort because it lets us believe that if we just removed one player, the game would be fair. But the game itself is the problem. The board is rigged. The rules are written by the people who own the pieces. And there are dozens of players — defense contractors, banks, pharmaceutical giants, oil companies, tech monopolies, foreign governments of every stripe — all shopping at the same market.

Israel is not the puppet master. It is a very good customer in a store that sells influence to anyone with money.

That’s the real story. And it’s worse than the myth.

The corruption we don’t call corruption

When we hear the word “corruption,” we picture a politician taking an envelope of cash in a parking garage. That happens, but it’s rare, and it’s illegal. The corruption that actually runs the world is legal. It’s normalized. It’s built into the architecture.

Here’s how it works:

  • Running for office costs a fortune. Politicians depend on donors, bundlers, PACs, and party machines.
  • Lobbyists write drafts of legislation and hand them to staffers.
  • Former officials walk through the revolving door into lobbying firms and boardrooms.
  • Dark money floods primaries and shapes public debate before a single vote is cast.
  • Think tanks, funded by the same interests, publish the “expert analysis” that frames the conversation.

None of this is secret. Most of it is perfectly legal. That’s the point.

And here’s where your standard matters. You said: taking a better-paying job while knowing it will make the world a bit shittier — that is corruption. I agree. That’s a high bar, and most people fail it. But that’s exactly the rot. A staffer who leaves Capitol Hill to become a lobbyist for a weapons manufacturer isn’t breaking the law. They’re just making a rational choice for their family. And the world gets a little worse.

The corruption isn’t the exception. It’s the operating system. Multiply the small, legal, individual choice by tens of thousands of people, across every industry, in every Western capital, and you have a system that runs on quiet, legal decisions that add up to collective ruin.

Money buys culture, too

Money doesn’t just buy votes. It buys the ground on which votes are cast.

It buys think tanks that define “serious” policy. It buys media outlets that set the boundaries of debate. It buys university chairs and research centers. It buys advertising, which buys attention, which buys consent. It buys the language we use — which conflicts are “crises” and which are “genocides,” whose suffering is “tragic” and whose is “complex.”

And yes — it buys the rules about what we’re allowed to say.

On speech laws: Antisemitism is a genuine hatred that has caused real horror, and it must be fought. But when legitimate criticism of a state’s policies gets branded as hatred of a people, something has gone wrong. When laws designed to protect a community are used to silence debate about a government, the effect is to place one country’s actions beyond the reach of normal political argument.

I am not in favour of antisemitism. I am against all hate speech, and I believe laws should be universal, not targeted. These laws are in contradiction with the charter of human rights. Why is there no such law against the French or First Nations? They have historical grievances too. We should not add more laws. We should remove the exception.

That’s not unique to Israel. It’s a pattern. Money and power always try to define the boundaries of acceptable speech. Sometimes they do it with lobbying. Sometimes with litigation. Sometimes with legislation. The mechanism varies. The goal is the same: make criticism costly.

And when criticism becomes costly, most people stop criticizing. Not because they’ve been convinced, but because they’ve been exhausted.

The system is general-purpose

Here’s the part the myth misses: the machinery that makes Israel effective is the same machinery that makes defense contractors, pharmaceutical companies, banks, and oil giants effective. It’s not a Jewish lobby that captured the West. It’s a market for influence that anyone with money can enter.

Defense

Contractors

Fund think tanks that write papers about “strategic necessity.” Lobby for budgets. Keep the revolving door spinning.

Pharma

Drug giants

Hire former regulators to advise on drug pricing. Spend billions on lobbying to shape the rules that govern them.

Finance

Banks

Spend fortunes on lobbying and campaign contributions. Shape the rules that govern their own behavior.

Energy

Oil and gas

Fund campaigns to cast doubt on climate science. Protect subsidies and drilling rights.

Foreign governments of all kinds — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Turkey, China, Russia — spend fortunes on lobbyists, public relations, and political donations to buy goodwill and access. Israel is very good at this game. But so are plenty of others. The game is the problem.

And the game has a name: legalized corruption. A system where money is speech, where access is for sale, where the revolving door spins freely, and where the people who write the rules are the people who benefit from them. Both parties serve the same donor class because both parties need the same money. The debate between them is real, but it happens within boundaries set by the people who fund them.

The greed that eats us from inside

So here is the harder truth. The problem is not one specific corruption. It’s not Israel. It’s not AIPAC. It’s not the military-industrial complex or Big Pharma or Wall Street, though all of them are players. The problem is corruption itself — the greed that eats us from the inside.

It starts with the individual choice. The better-paying job. The consulting gig. The board seat. The campaign donation you accept because you need it to win. The story you tell yourself: Everyone does it. I’m just being practical. I can do more good from the inside.

And maybe you can. But the system has heard that excuse a million times, and it has learned to digest it. It turns good intentions into lubricant. It makes compromise feel like realism. It makes greed feel like responsibility.

Then it scales up. The individual choice becomes institutional policy. The institutional policy becomes national strategy. And the national strategy becomes a war — fought by people who never made the choice, paid for by people who never voted for it, and justified by people who never believed the justification.

Mankind at war with itself. Not because one group of people is evil and another is good. But because the operating system rewards the worst in us — the fear, the greed, the desire to dominate — and then calls it realism.

We are not fighting a war between nations. We are fighting a war between the parts of ourselves that want to build and the parts that want to take. And right now, the takers are winning. Not because they’re stronger. Because the system is built for them.

The real enemy

So let’s retire the myth. Israel does not control the West. The West has been sold — piece by piece, law by law, election by election — to the highest bidders. Israel is one bidder. There are many others. The auction never stops.

The real enemy is not a country or a lobby or a party. It’s the logic that says everything is for sale. It’s the greed that says I’ll take mine, and the world can burn. It’s the corruption that begins in the small, reasonable, legal choices we all make — and ends in a world where the people who profit from destruction decide which destruction comes next.

The problem is corruption itself. The greed that eats us from the inside. The thing that pushes us to kill each other so we can decide who gets to wreck the rest of the planet faster.

That’s the war. It’s not between Israel and Palestine, or Russia and Ukraine, or America and China. Those are symptoms. The war is between those who want a world that works for everyone and those who want a world that works for them. And until we name that war clearly — until we stop looking for a single villain and start looking at the system — we will keep losing it.

The first step is to stop believing in the myth of control. There is no secret cabal. There is only a market. And we are all — every one of us — either bidding, or being sold.

Money buys access. Access shapes policy. Policy becomes war. War becomes profit. Profit buys more access. The loop closes. The only way out is to name the loop — and refuse to feed it.

Mankind at War with Itself — an essay on corruption, greed, and the myth of control.

Released for use, criticism, and revision. It is a hypothesis, not a doctrine.
